HEALTHCARE KNOWLEDGE BASE

==================================================
1. GENERAL HEALTHCARE
==================================================

Healthcare is the prevention, diagnosis, treatment, and management of diseases,
illnesses, injuries, and other health conditions. Healthcare can include
preventive care, primary care, emergency care, specialist care, rehabilitation,
and long-term disease management.

Preventive healthcare focuses on reducing the risk of illness before a disease
develops. Examples include vaccinations, health screenings, regular medical
checkups, maintaining a balanced diet, physical activity, adequate sleep, and
avoiding tobacco products.

Primary care is usually the first point of contact for many health concerns.
Primary care professionals can evaluate common illnesses, provide preventive
care, manage chronic conditions, and refer patients to specialists when needed.

==================================================
2. DIABETES
==================================================

Diabetes is a chronic metabolic condition involving elevated blood glucose
levels. Blood glucose is regulated in part by insulin, a hormone produced by
the pancreas.

Type 1 diabetes occurs when the immune system destroys insulin-producing beta
cells in the pancreas. People with type 1 diabetes generally require insulin
therapy to manage blood glucose.

Type 2 diabetes occurs when the body becomes resistant to insulin and/or does
not produce enough insulin. Risk factors can include family history, excess
body weight, physical inactivity, age, and certain other medical conditions.

Common symptoms of diabetes may include increased thirst, frequent urination,
increased hunger, fatigue, blurred vision, and unexplained weight changes.
Some people with type 2 diabetes may have few or no noticeable symptoms.

Long-term poorly controlled diabetes can affect blood vessels and nerves and
increase the risk of complications involving the eyes, kidneys, heart,
and nervous system.

Diabetes management can involve monitoring blood glucose, nutrition,
physical activity, medications, and regular medical follow-up.

==================================================
3. HYPERTENSION
==================================================

Hypertension, commonly called high blood pressure, occurs when blood pressure
remains higher than the recommended range over time.

Blood pressure is expressed using two numbers. The systolic pressure represents
pressure when the heart contracts, while diastolic pressure represents pressure
when the heart relaxes.

Hypertension often does not cause noticeable symptoms. Because of this, regular
blood pressure measurement is important.

Long-term uncontrolled hypertension can increase the risk of cardiovascular
disease, stroke, kidney disease, and other complications.

Lifestyle measures that may help manage blood pressure include reducing sodium
intake, maintaining a healthy weight, being physically active, avoiding tobacco,
limiting alcohol, managing stress, and getting adequate sleep.

Some people require prescription medications to control blood pressure.

==================================================
4. HEART DISEASE
==================================================

Cardiovascular disease refers to conditions affecting the heart and blood
vessels.

Coronary artery disease occurs when plaque builds up in the arteries that supply
blood to the heart muscle. This can reduce blood flow and may contribute to
chest discomfort or a heart attack.

A heart attack occurs when blood flow to part of the heart muscle is blocked
or severely reduced. Possible symptoms include chest pressure or discomfort,
shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, and discomfort that may spread to the
arm, shoulder, back, neck, or jaw.

Heart failure occurs when the heart cannot pump blood sufficiently to meet the
body's needs. Symptoms may include shortness of breath, fatigue, and swelling
in the legs or ankles.

Risk factors for cardiovascular disease include high blood pressure, diabetes,
high cholesterol, smoking, physical inactivity, obesity, age, and family
history.

==================================================
5. ASTHMA
==================================================

Asthma is a chronic condition affecting the airways. The airways can become
inflamed and narrowed, making breathing difficult.

Common asthma symptoms include wheezing, coughing, chest tightness, and
shortness of breath.

Asthma symptoms can be triggered by allergens, respiratory infections, smoke,
air pollution, exercise, cold air, and other environmental factors.

Asthma treatment can include quick-relief medicines for acute symptoms and
long-term controller medicines for people who need them.

An asthma action plan can help patients recognize worsening symptoms and know
when to use prescribed treatments or seek medical care.

==================================================
6. RESPIRATORY INFECTIONS
==================================================

Respiratory infections affect parts of the respiratory system such as the nose,
throat, airways, or lungs.

The common cold is usually caused by viruses and may cause a runny nose,
congestion, cough, sore throat, and mild fatigue.

Influenza, commonly called the flu, is a viral respiratory illness that can
cause fever, cough, sore throat, muscle aches, headache, chills, and fatigue.

Pneumonia is an infection that causes inflammation in the lungs. Symptoms may
include cough, fever, chills, chest discomfort, and difficulty breathing.

Antibiotics treat certain bacterial infections but do not treat viral
infections such as the common cold.

==================================================
7. NUTRITION
==================================================

Good nutrition provides the body with energy and nutrients required for growth,
repair, and normal physiological functions.

Major nutrients include carbohydrates, proteins, and fats. Vitamins and
minerals are micronutrients required in smaller quantities.

A balanced eating pattern can include vegetables, fruits, whole grains,
protein-rich foods, and appropriate sources of healthy fats.

Adequate hydration is important for normal body functions. Water needs vary
depending on factors such as activity level, climate, diet, and individual
health conditions.

Excessive consumption of highly processed foods, added sugars, and sodium may
increase health risks in some individuals.

==================================================
8. OBESITY AND HEALTHY WEIGHT
==================================================

Obesity is a chronic health condition involving excess body fat that can
increase the risk of several diseases.

Factors contributing to body weight can include genetics, eating patterns,
physical activity, sleep, medications, environment, and socioeconomic factors.

Obesity is associated with an increased risk of conditions such as type 2
diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, sleep apnea, and certain
other health conditions.

Healthy weight management generally involves sustainable eating habits,
regular physical activity, adequate sleep, and appropriate medical support
when needed.

==================================================
9. MENTAL HEALTH
==================================================

Mental health refers to emotional, psychological, and social well-being.

Mental health conditions can affect mood, thoughts, behavior, relationships,
work, education, and daily activities.

Depression can involve persistent sadness or loss of interest along with
changes in sleep, appetite, energy, concentration, or feelings of worth.

Anxiety disorders can involve excessive fear, worry, or anxiety that interferes
with daily activities.

Mental health treatment can include psychotherapy, medication when appropriate,
support from healthcare professionals, lifestyle changes, and social support.

If someone is experiencing a mental health emergency or is at immediate risk
of harming themselves or others, emergency services or an appropriate crisis
service should be contacted.

==================================================
10. SLEEP
==================================================

Sleep is important for physical health, brain function, memory, mood, and
overall well-being.

Insufficient sleep can affect concentration, reaction time, mood, and physical
health.

Good sleep habits can include maintaining a consistent sleep schedule, keeping
the sleeping environment comfortable and dark, limiting stimulating activities
before bedtime, and avoiding excessive caffeine late in the day.

Persistent difficulty sleeping or excessive daytime sleepiness may require
evaluation by a healthcare professional.

==================================================
11. PHYSICAL ACTIVITY
==================================================

Regular physical activity supports cardiovascular health, muscle strength,
bone health, metabolic health, and mental well-being.

Physical activity can include walking, cycling, swimming, running, strength
training, sports, and other forms of movement.

People who have been inactive can generally begin with manageable activities
and gradually increase duration or intensity according to their abilities.

Individuals with certain medical conditions may need guidance from a healthcare
professional before beginning a new exercise program.

==================================================
12. CHOLESTEROL
==================================================

Cholesterol is a waxy substance needed by the body for certain biological
functions. It is transported through the bloodstream by lipoproteins.

Low-density lipoprotein, commonly called LDL, is often described as
"bad cholesterol" because high levels can contribute to plaque formation in
arteries.

High-density lipoprotein, or HDL, is often called "good cholesterol" because
it participates in transporting cholesterol away from tissues.

High cholesterol usually does not cause noticeable symptoms. A blood test is
used to measure cholesterol levels.

Management can include dietary changes, physical activity, weight management,
and medications when prescribed.

==================================================
13. KIDNEY HEALTH
==================================================

The kidneys filter waste products and excess fluid from the blood and help
maintain electrolyte and fluid balance.

Chronic kidney disease occurs when kidney function or kidney structure is
abnormal for a prolonged period.

Diabetes and high blood pressure are major causes of chronic kidney disease.

Kidney disease may progress without obvious symptoms during its early stages.
Blood and urine tests can help evaluate kidney function and detect kidney
damage.

Managing conditions such as diabetes and hypertension can help reduce the risk
of kidney damage.

==================================================
14. LIVER HEALTH
==================================================

The liver performs many important functions including processing nutrients,
producing proteins, and helping the body process various substances.

Liver diseases can have many causes, including viral infections, alcohol,
metabolic conditions, medications, and other factors.

Fatty liver disease involves excess fat accumulation in the liver. Metabolic
factors such as obesity and type 2 diabetes can increase its risk.

Some liver conditions may not cause symptoms during their early stages.

==================================================
15. IMMUNE SYSTEM
==================================================

The immune system protects the body against harmful organisms and abnormal
cells.

White blood cells are important components of the immune response.

Vaccines help train the immune system to recognize specific infectious agents
and can reduce the risk of certain diseases and their complications.

An immune response can produce inflammation. Inflammation is an important part
of the body's defense system, although excessive or inappropriate inflammation
can contribute to disease.

==================================================
16. INFECTION PREVENTION
==================================================

Hand hygiene is an important measure for reducing the spread of infectious
organisms.

Vaccination is an important public health strategy for preventing many
infectious diseases.

Respiratory hygiene measures, appropriate ventilation, staying home when sick,
and following healthcare guidance can reduce transmission of respiratory
infections.

Healthcare facilities use infection-control procedures to reduce the spread
of infectious organisms between patients and healthcare workers.

==================================================
17. FIRST AID
==================================================

First aid refers to immediate assistance provided to someone who is injured
or suddenly becomes ill before professional medical care is available.

For serious emergencies, contacting local emergency services should take
priority.

For severe bleeding, applying firm direct pressure with appropriate material
can help control bleeding while emergency assistance is obtained.

For suspected fractures, the injured area should generally be kept as still as
possible while professional medical care is sought.

Burns should be removed from the source of heat when safe, and appropriate
first-aid measures should be used depending on the severity.

==================================================
18. MEDICATION SAFETY
==================================================

Medicines should be used according to instructions from a healthcare
professional or the official product information.

Patients should tell healthcare professionals about medicines, supplements,
and allergies that may be relevant to their care.

Taking more medicine than prescribed can be dangerous.

Medication should generally not be shared with other people.

Some medicines can interact with other medicines, foods, supplements, or
medical conditions. Patients should ask a healthcare professional or pharmacist
about potential interactions.

==================================================
19. HEALTH SCREENING
==================================================

Health screening involves checking for certain diseases or risk factors before
symptoms appear.

Examples of screening programs can include blood pressure measurement,
cholesterol testing, diabetes screening, cancer screening, and other tests
depending on age, risk factors, sex, family history, and medical guidance.

Screening recommendations vary between individuals and populations.

A healthcare professional can help determine which screenings are appropriate
for a particular person.

==================================================
20. EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS
==================================================

Some symptoms require urgent medical evaluation.

Possible emergency warning signs can include severe difficulty breathing,
severe chest pain or pressure, sudden weakness or numbness, sudden difficulty
speaking, severe allergic reactions, uncontrolled severe bleeding, loss of
consciousness, or other rapidly worsening symptoms.

In a medical emergency, contact the appropriate local emergency service or
seek emergency medical care.
